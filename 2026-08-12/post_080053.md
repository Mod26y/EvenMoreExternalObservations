Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/delta-probes-wi-fi-deauth-attack-on-flight-carrying-def-con-attendees/

1) What happened: An unauthorized Wi-Fi network was discovered onboard a Delta Air Lines flight from Las Vegas to Atlanta, carrying passengers from the DEF CON hacker convention. This incident involved a Wi-Fi deauthentication attack where passengers were disconnected from the aircraft's in-flight Wi-Fi network. Reports suggest that a rogue network, mimicking Delta's network, was broadcasted, potentially to harvest personal credentials and Google login data. Federal authorities intervened once the plane landed, questioning suspects and seizing hardware linked to the attack.

2) Why it matters: This incident highlights the vulnerabilities in in-flight Wi-Fi systems, which could be exploited for malicious purposes, including phishing or denial-of-service attacks. As airlines provide Wi-Fi for passenger convenience, ensuring its security is crucial to protect against data breaches and preserve passenger trust. The attack also demonstrates that even a seemingly secure and constrained environment like an aircraft can be targeted, emphasizing the importance of robust cybersecurity measures in aviation.

3) What actions should be taken: Airlines should bolster their in-flight network security by implementing measures such as Protected Management Frames (PMF) to defend against deauthentication attacks. Regular audits and robust encryption can prevent unauthorized network creation and data interception. Collaborative efforts with cybersecurity experts, including post-conference security assessments, can further secure networks against sophisticated threats. Additionally, educating airline staff and passengers about identifying phishing Wi-Fi networks enhances overall security awareness and can help mitigate potential attacks.
