Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/attackers-conceal-phishing-lures-using-invisible-unicode-characters/

1) This article discusses a phishing campaign where threat actors use "ASCII smuggling" to hide malicious code using invisible Unicode characters. This technique, observed by Microsoft, involves inserting these characters into finance-related words to bypass email security filters. A large campaign peaked in February 2023, with up to 2.37 million daily phishing emails, and although its scale reduced by May, it remains active. The campaign primarily used finance-themed domains and targeted users by promoting financial services, leveraging infrastructure provided by legitimate email marketing platforms.

2) This matters because it highlights the evolving tactics of cybercriminals who continuously seek new methods to evade traditional security measures. The use of invisible Unicode characters poses a significant threat, undermining the effectiveness of keyword-based security systems. As attackers adapt, so must defense strategies, particularly in financial services where phishing attacks can lead to significant data and monetary loss. The campaign's use of legitimate platforms for message delivery complicates detection and response efforts for businesses and individuals alike.

3) Organizations should consider updating their email security systems to identify and mitigate threats involving Unicode characters. Implementing recommendations from Microsoft, such as normalizing email content to remove or flag invisible characters before applying textual analysis, can enhance detection capabilities. Organizations should also review and enhance AI system inputs to prevent prompt-injection attacks. Additionally, fostering user awareness and training on recognizing phishing attempts remains crucial in combatting these sophisticated techniques. Regular monitoring and collaboration with email service providers can improve overall security posture against such threats.
