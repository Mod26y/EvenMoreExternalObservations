**1) What happened:** Although the specific details of the article aren't available, the title "CSS: The Hidden Threat Lurking in Your Inbox" suggests that attackers may be leveraging Cascading Style Sheets (CSS) in emails to execute malicious activities. This likely involves embedding malicious code or exploiting vulnerabilities in email clients to compromise user systems, potentially bypassing traditional security measures.

**2) Why it matters:** CSS in emails could be an overlooked vector for cyberattacks, highlighting a new method for threat actors to infiltrate networks. CSS-based threats could evade standard email security solutions and exploit vulnerabilities within email clients, increasing the risk of data breaches and other cyber incidents. Awareness of such threats is essential to bolster defenses against evolving attack techniques.

**3) What actions should be taken:** Organizations may consider enhancing email security protocols by integrating advanced threat detection that scrutinizes embedded styles and scripts. Security awareness training for employees could cover identifying suspicious emails with unusual CSS use. Regular updates and patches for email clients and related software are advisable to mitigate vulnerabilities that such attacks might exploit. Implementing these measures can help in proactively managing risks associated with this threat.
