In this article, CISA and international partners emphasize the need for critical infrastructure organizations to prepare for cyberattacks by isolating operational technology (OT) systems. The guidance is a response to ongoing threats from state-sponsored and cybercriminal actors targeting essential services like water, energy, and telecommunications. It includes methods for physically or gradually isolating vital systems from less-secure networks, which is crucial to maintaining essential services during a cyber event. The backdrop of incidents, such as breaches by the Chinese Volt Typhoon group, highlights the urgent need for thorough preparation and defensive measures.

The significance of these developments lies in the increasing frequency and sophistication of attacks on critical infrastructure, which pose severe risks to public safety, economic stability, and national security. By isolating OT systems during an attack, organizations can limit potential damage, protect sensitive data, and ensure continuity of essential services. These capabilities are crucial, especially given the systemic reliance on interdependent digital systems and the inherent vulnerability of infrastructure to disruptive cyber events, as evidenced by recent breaches across various sectors.

In response to this guidance, organizations should proactively assess and document their OT networks and dependencies. Establishing isolation points, familiarizing personnel with isolation procedures, and regularly testing these measures are vital initial steps. Beyond testing, entities can look to ensure that both digital resilience and manual operations can function independently when necessary. Organizations are encouraged to secure offline copies of isolation plans, maintain robust monitoring for unauthorized connections post-isolation, and address risks related to disconnected systems, such as patch management challenges.
