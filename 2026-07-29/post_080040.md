**What Happened:**

A breach at the University of Pennsylvania demonstrated vulnerabilities in the Single Sign-On (SSO) system when a compromised account was used to access multiple internal systems, resulting in data theft affecting 1.2 million individuals. SSO simplifies access by allowing users to authenticate across multiple systems with one set of credentials, but this also magnifies the impact if those credentials are compromised. The incident highlights the importance of proper configuration and protection of SSO systems.

**Why It Matters:**

SSO systems centralize authentication processes, making them a critical target for attackers. The Penn incident shows that while SSO systems can enhance security by reducing the need for multiple passwords and simplifying access control, they also pose a significant risk if not properly managed. The potential for large-scale access from a single credential breach underscores the need for robust security measures in protecting SSO credentials to prevent unauthorized access to sensitive data and systems.

**What Actions Should Be Taken:**

Organizations should enforce strong password policies, following NIST's guidance on length and usability, and implement multi-factor authentication (MFA) robustly across all access scenarios. It is critical to adopt phishing-resistant MFA methods such as FIDO2 keys or WebAuthn for high-risk accounts. Tight control over identity provider administrators, signing certificates, and OAuth credentials is essential to prevent misuse. Regular reviews of permissions, consent grants, and identity management policies can prevent improper access and maintain the integrity of the SSO system.
