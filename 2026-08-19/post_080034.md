Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/your-controls-block-known-attacks-what-about-the-behavior/

1) The article highlights issues in enterprise cybersecurity effectiveness based on findings from the Blue Report 2026 by Picus Labs. Picus Labs ran over 338 million attack simulations to gauge how well enterprises defend against cyber threats. While prevention rates have increased to 69%, the report reveals significant weaknesses. Signature-based methods fail to recognize less conspicuous threat behaviors, like alternative paths used by tools such as Mimikatz, resulting in drastically lower prevention rates for these variants. Once attackers breach the perimeter, internal defenses only block about 37% of malicious actions.

2) This information matters as it underscores a critical issue in cybersecurity: reliance on signature-based detection is proving inadequate against evolving attack methods. Organizations may falsely believe they are secure while sophisticated attacks exploit unrecognized pathways, leading to credential theft and unauthorized access. Enhanced understanding of both IOC and TTP-based methods is vital. Comprehensive security strategies that focus on behavior rather than solely procedures are critical to close this gap and fortify against attacks that traditional methods overlook.

3) Organizations should enhance their cybersecurity infrastructure by adopting a dual-layer testing strategy: Known-bad testing should verify perimeter defenses, while behavioral validation should test endpoint and internal controls against various attack behaviors. Utilizing tools like Picus Swarm can automate this process, ensuring thorough coverage without needing to expand the stack significantly. By focusing on understanding and mitigating vulnerabilities in existing systems, organizations can make informed decisions to patch, monitor, or potentially accept certain risks with documented evidence. This proactive stance aids in maintaining robust defenses while optimizing current investments.
