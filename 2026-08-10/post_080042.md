The article discusses two distinct cybersecurity attack chains identified in the first half of 2026. The first campaign involved banking malware using compromised corporate mailboxes to deliver legitimate-looking emails, which led recipients to execute malicious JavaScript and PowerShell scripts. This eventually manipulated victims’ proxy and browser settings during banking sessions. The second campaign focused on cryptocurrency transactions, where a clipboard hijacker written in Rust altered copied wallet addresses, rerouting payments to attacker-controlled addresses. Both campaigns effectively compromised user trust without breaching the core systems and relied on users' unawareness during trustworthy interactions.

These incidents emphasize the importance of monitoring both the integrity of email accounts and clipboard operations. The misuse of legitimate email systems to deliver malicious content threatens the perceived trust in business communications, particularly affecting regions like Czechia and Slovakia. Additionally, the cryptocurrency attack highlights the vulnerability of users who rely on clipboard functions for financial transactions. With attackers utilizing trusted infrastructures and user activities as attack vectors, traditional methods of detection and reliance on human vigilance are challenged. The implications are serious for both financial and reputational safety.

To mitigate these threats, organizations should enhance monitoring of email authentication mechanisms and follow through with post-delivery assessments of suspicious activity sequences, combining tools to detect script execution and browsing anomalies. For protecting against clipboard hijacking, implementing controls that monitor for unauthorized address changes or unusual blockchain queries can be beneficial. Educating users to carefully verify transaction details, particularly with cryptocurrency transfers, helps prevent address spoofing. Utilizing address books or lists of approved addresses can further reduce risk, fostering a more secure transaction environment.
