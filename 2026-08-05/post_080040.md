Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/77-open-vsx-extensions-found-harvesting-developer-info/

The "evil twin" campaign involved 77 malicious extensions on the Open VSX marketplace that mimicked legitimate developer tools to covertly harvest information about the systems and development environments they were installed on. The extensions were identified by Manifold Security and shared a data-exfiltration domain indicating coordinated activity. While many extensions only exfiltrated basic system information, a subset collected detailed developer and CI metadata.

This matter because the exfiltration of development-related metadata could facilitate further targeted attacks against organizations, impacting their cybersecurity posture. Even though source code and credentials were not accessed, the data harvested could help an attacker understand an organization's development ecosystem and potentially exploit other vulnerabilities. The implications extend to trust issues in marketplace ecosystems and highlight the ongoing risk of software supply chain attacks.

As a preventive measure, organizations should audit their systems for the malicious extension IDs and manually remove them if detected. Blocking the domain mangorbit[.]com and similar variants is advisable to prevent data exfiltration. An ongoing review of marketplace extensions for similar threats and using network security tools to detect suspicious activity related to development environments helps enhance an organization’s protective measures against such campaigns.
