Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/akira-hackers-disable-edr-with-safe-mode-steal-data-but-fail-to-encrypt/

1) What happened: The Akira ransomware gang exploited a SonicWall VPN device without MFA to gain initial access to a system. After entering, they maneuvered through the network, stealing data before reverting the system to Safe Mode—a startup setting that disables most third-party software, including endpoint detection and response (EDR) systems. The attackers used tools like WinRAR and s5cmd for data exfiltration and AnyDesk for sustained access. However, their attempt to execute the ransomware failed due to system errors. Despite not encrypting the files, they successfully stole credentials and data for extortion purposes.

2) Why it matters: This incident highlights the vulnerabilities present when multi-factor authentication (MFA) is not utilized, especially in critical access points like VPNs. Disabling security measures in Safe Mode demonstrates a tactical advancement, allowing cyber criminals to maneuver undetected initially. With the attackers managing to steal sensitive data rapidly, this showcases how gaps in security protocols can lead to swift exploitation by organized threat actors, necessitating vigilant reinforcement of security policies.

3) What actions should be taken: Organizations should implement MFA on all VPN accounts to bolster defenses against unauthorized access. Monitoring for unusual changes to Safe Mode configurations and the installation of remote-access tools can provide early indicators of such intrusions. Lastly, employing credential-spraying detection and routine security audits can help identify and address potential vulnerabilities, making it more difficult for attackers to exploit privileged credentials and navigate within the network.
