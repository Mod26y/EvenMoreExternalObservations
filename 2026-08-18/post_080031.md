Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/certighost-and-the-privilege-hiding-in-your-certificate-authority/

1) **What happened:**
The article discusses a vulnerability, Certighost (CVE-2026-54121), within Microsoft's Active Directory Certificate Services (AD CS). It enables a low-privileged Active Directory user to impersonate a Domain Controller by exploiting a flaw in the CA's "chase" functionality. This allows the attacker to obtain a certificate with Domain Controller privileges, leveraging it to potentially take control of the domain. Microsoft released a patch on July 14, 2026, to address this significant issue in their public key infrastructure that is critical for authentication and secure communications across the network.

2) **Why it matters:**
The Certighost vulnerability highlights the critical nature of trust relationships and privilege management in cybersecurity. Certificate Authorities, which issue and manage digital certificates, are pivotal in maintaining secure communications and authentication flows. When these trusted entities are exploited, it exposes an entire system to potential takeover by unprivileged users. The flaw underscores a broader issue of latent privileges and default configurations in Active Directory environments, which can be leveraged by attackers. Effective trust and privilege management is crucial for safeguarding against such identity and domain compromise risks.

3) **What actions should be taken:**
Organizations should prioritize applying the Microsoft patch for Certighost to eliminate the specific vulnerability. In the interim, disabling the chase functionality can serve as a temporary safeguard, though it requires careful testing to avoid disrupting legitimate operations. Additionally, reducing the MachineAccountQuota to zero can mitigate the risk of misuse in domain environments by default users. Beyond immediate fixes, entities should audit and restrain CA communications to only trusted Domain Controllers, review certificate templates and enrollment permissions, and bolster monitoring for anomalous activities that signal identity abuse. Continuous trust validation and limiting standing privileges are fundamental steps to prevent future vulnerabilities.
