Evooo1Bot is a new Linux botnet based on Mirai malware that transforms internet-facing routers into SOCKS5 traffic relay nodes. Beyond proxying, Evooo1Bot can steal credentials, perform SSH brute-force attacks, and execute distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks. It primarily targets devices from manufacturers like D-Link, NETGEAR, and Alcatel by exploiting known vulnerabilities. The botnet uses a modular design with encrypted command-and-control communications and various persistence mechanisms, such as systemd and cron jobs. Despite some incomplete exploit implementations, its numerous features make it a significant threat.

The emergence of Evooo1Bot is significant because it highlights vulnerabilities in internet gateway devices, potentially impacting millions of users. By converting routers and network devices into proxy nodes, attackers can mask malicious actions and evade geographical restrictions. This broadens the attack surface and creates opportunities for large-scale DDoS attacks, data theft, and network breaches. The threat emphasizes the consequences of unpatched devices and weak authentication practices in both enterprises and personal use cases.

Organizations should prioritize updating firmware on IoT devices and replacing them when no longer supported by vendors. Admin credentials should be regularly changed from defaults, and remote access should be disabled to reduce exposure. Businesses can improve defenses by implementing comprehensive logging to detect unusual traffic patterns and deploying network segmentation to limit lateral movement. Continuous monitoring and regular security audits could also facilitate proactive threat management, reducing susceptibility to botnets and related attacks.
