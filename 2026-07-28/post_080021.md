Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/new-dysphoria-ddos-botnet-spreads-to-200k-devices-worldwide/

**What Happened:** The Dysphoria botnet has compromised approximately 200,000 devices globally, exploiting them for DDoS attacks and traffic relay tasks. It evolved from previous malware, incorporating a blockchain-based command-and-control mechanism that makes it difficult to trace and dismantle. The botnet disseminates via weak Telnet and SSH credentials and exploits both recent and older vulnerabilities in routers, cameras, and IoT devices. The botnet claims a significant DDoS capacity, making it a notable threat despite not reaching the highest recorded attack figures.

**Why It Matters:** The increase in botnet-infected devices signifies a growing threat landscape, especially with the botnet's capability to execute potent DDoS attacks. Its resilience and ability to adapt through updates challenge cybersecurity defenses. Moreover, the use of blockchain technology for command and control complicates traditional disruption tactics. This situation underscores the urgent need for strengthened cybersecurity measures, as these botnets can cause substantial operational disruptions and service outages, impacting businesses and critical infrastructure.

**Actions to Be Taken:** Devices should be secured by keeping firmware updated and altering default credentials for enhanced protection. Disabling unnecessary remote access and tightening security settings where feasible can mitigate infection risks. Organizations should prioritize regular vulnerability assessments and employ advanced detection strategies, such as breach and attack simulations, to promptly identify and respond to potential intrusions. Continued vigilance and adaptation to emerging threats are crucial for maintaining robust cybersecurity postures. Additionally, collaboration with cybersecurity experts can aid in crafting effective countermeasures and promoting awareness and readiness against such botnet threats.
