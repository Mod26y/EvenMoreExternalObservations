1) The article likely emphasizes that cyber adversaries are increasingly exploiting known vulnerabilities and understanding the common security practices organizations use, rather than relying on sophisticated zero-day exploits. These adversaries study security frameworks and systems to find gaps and leverage the predictable nature of many security measures, which makes it easier for them to bypass defenses and cause potential data breaches or network compromises.

2) This issue is critical because it highlights how even well-established security practices can be circumvented if they're too rigid or predictable. Organizations tend to over-rely on traditional forms of defense, assuming that adhering to standard frameworks is entirely secure. When adversaries exploit this predictability, it exposes the organization to risks without needing cutting-edge exploits, which broadens the pool of potential attackers since less technical expertise is required.

3) In response, organizations should adopt a more dynamic and adaptive security strategy, continuously updating and diversifying their defenses. Regular penetration testing, threat simulations, and behavior-based detection systems can help identify and close the predictable security gaps. Staff should be trained to recognize non-standard threats and the importance of questioning the status quo of existing security measures. Additionally, fostering an organizational culture open to revising and customizing security protocols based on emerging threats will be crucial.
