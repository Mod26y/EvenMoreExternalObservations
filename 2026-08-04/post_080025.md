Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/new-doublecup-clickfix-service-hides-malware-in-browser-cache-images/

**1) What happened:**  
A recently uncovered Russian cybercriminal service, DOUBLECUP, offers loader-as-a-service functionality using ClickFix attacks to hide malware within PNG images cached by browser sessions. By exploiting steganographic techniques, DOUBLECUP masks its payloads which include an updated CountLoader for both Windows and macOS, and a new remote access trojan (RAT) referred to as DeviceManager on Windows systems. This service has been operating since June 2026, facilitating the technical infrastructure for criminal operators to execute malicious campaigns targeting major browsers with fake CAPTCHA prompts on prominent platform login pages.

**2) Why it matters:**  
The emergence of DOUBLECUP represents a significant threat evolution as it combines steganography with a sophisticated delivery mechanism to target widely-used software platforms. The attack chain is particularly concerning for its capability to bypass traditional security defenses by embedding in legitimate-looking prompts and leveraging browser caches discreetly. Such methods make detection and mitigation difficult, increasing the risk of data breaches and malware infections that could harvest sensitive information or allow attackers remote control of systems. Cybersecurity frameworks need enhancement to detect embedded threats within benign-looking digital content.

**3) What actions should be taken as a result of this information:**  
Organizations should strengthen their incident response capabilities and enhance endpoint protection to detect anomalies such as unauthorized clipboard activity or unexpected image downloads. Implementing sophisticated threat intelligence and monitoring tools to recognize the unique patterns and signs of ClickFix-style attacks is advisable. Enhanced user awareness programs highlighting the risks of pasting content from unknown sources can mitigate initial infection vectors. Additionally, reviewing and updating web application security protocols to identify and block obfuscated scripts can help prevent DOUBLECUP-related attacks.
