Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hotel-wi-fi-attacks-use-custom-malware-to-breach-microsoft-365-accounts/

1) What happened: A global cyberattack campaign, named CaptiveCrunch, has targeted hospitality Wi-Fi networks to breach Microsoft 365 accounts. The campaign has been attributed to the Russian threat actor APT29, or Midnight Blizzard, and involves manipulating DNS settings on Wi-Fi devices to redirect users to phishing pages. Two malware families, CornFlake, and ChocoShell, have been identified, which enable various malicious activities including credential theft and surveillance. The attackers intercepted traffic by exploiting captive portal equipment, and were also seen distributing malware to Windows and Android devices.

2) Why it matters: This campaign highlights the vulnerability of public Wi-Fi networks, particularly in hotels and conference centers where personal and corporate accounts are at risk of compromise. The threat actors effectively exploit common technologies, impacting not only individual users but potentially larger organizations through compromised Microsoft 365 accounts. Given the widespread use of public Wi-Fi and Microsoft 365, the attack underscores the need for robust cybersecurity measures, emphasizing the ever-present risk from state-sponsored groups.

3) What actions should be taken: Individuals and organizations should treat public Wi-Fi networks as potentially untrusted environments. Using private cellular networks or secure, managed connections significantly mitigates risk. It would be prudent to avoid software updates through captive portals, adopt phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication, and disable unnecessary device code authentication features. Additionally, organizations should enhance user awareness regarding the secure handling of corporate credentials and regularly review and update security protocols to defend against emerging threats. Implementing thorough security testing and simulations can also help identify vulnerabilities in security systems.
