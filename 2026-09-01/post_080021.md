Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/microsoft-warns-of-terminalfix-attacks-deploying-reverse-tunnels/

The article discusses a new cyberattack tactic named TerminalFix, a variant of ClickFix, which uses fake CAPTCHA prompts to deliver malicious PowerShell scripts leading to multi-stage intrusion, ultimately granting attackers reverse tunnel access into victims’ internal networks. Unlike typical ClickFix attacks that often distribute infostealers, TerminalFix directs users to execute scripts allowing complex operations, including lateral movement within networks.

This is significant because it represents an evolution in intrusion tactics that not only exfiltrate data but also allow attackers to pivot within networks, potentially leading to broader compromise, including theft of credentials, privilege escalation, or deployment of ransomware. The use of steganography and reverse tunneling makes detection and mitigation more challenging, emphasizing the risk for organizations, especially those with insufficient monitoring and logging of PowerShell and other administrative tools.

In response to these threats, organizations should enhance PowerShell execution restrictions and logging, monitor for suspicious activity involving 'LockScreenContentServer.exe', and bolster endpoint protection and browser security. Post-compromise actions need to include investigating potential lateral movement, credential rotation, particularly for domain administrators, and examining network pivot points used by attackers. These measures will help mitigate the impact of TerminalFix and safeguard sensitive internal systems.
