1) The article discusses the implications of removing a root of trust from digital infrastructure. This situation often arises when a certificate authority (CA) is compromised or found untrustworthy, necessitating revocation to maintain system security. Root of trust forms the foundational security layer; its removal without a planned transition can cause widespread disruption as dependent systems and communications no longer have a reliable point of verification.

2) This issue is significant because a root of trust is integral for the authentication and integrity of digital communications. If not managed carefully, its revocation could lead to massive operational disruptions across businesses and governmental functions, potentially exposing them to cyber threats. Understanding how to manage these transitions effectively is crucial for maintaining the confidentiality and reliability of digital systems.

3) In response to the challenge posed by pulling a root of trust, organizations should focus on establishing robust contingency plans for such events. This could include adopting a layered approach to certificate management and maintaining up-to-date inventories of systems dependent on specific certificates. Additionally, engaging with cybersecurity experts to assess vulnerabilities and testing incident response plans through simulations will aid in minimizing disruptions and safeguarding data integrity during such transitions.
