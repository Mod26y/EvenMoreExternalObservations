Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/google/google-chrome-may-soon-block-new-tab-hijacker-extensions-by-default/

1) What happened: Google is developing a Chrome security feature to counteract unauthorized changes to the New Tab page or default search engine by extensions installed through local policies. This new measure, identified in Chromium Gerrit changes, would automatically block and save the extension ID of any unwanted policy-controlled extensions on unmanaged consumer devices, marking a shift towards tighter security controls to mitigate malware exploitation on Google Chrome.

2) Why it matters: This development is crucial as it targets a common exploitation vector that can lead to a multitude of security issues, including privacy breaches and exposure to malicious websites. By blocking these unauthorized changes, Google is aiming to enhance user security on unmanaged devices, which are susceptible to policy exploitation by malware. It underscores the ongoing challenges in maintaining digital security and the need for continued advancements in defensive measures to protect consumer data and browsing integrity.

3) What actions should be taken: Organizations and individual users should prepare for the upcoming changes by reviewing their existing Chrome configurations and extension usage. It's advisable to maintain good browser hygiene by regularly auditing installed extensions and educating users about potential risks. Organizations should evaluate their necessity for policy-enforced extensions and adjust practices to ensure compliance with future updates. Stay informed about the latest Chrome updates to understand potential impacts and leverage available tools and metrics to monitor for unauthorized extension behavior.
