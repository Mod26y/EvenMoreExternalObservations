Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hundreds-of-fake-chrome-vpn-extensions-route-traffic-through-a-proxy/

1. **What happened:** Over 737 fake browser extensions impersonated popular VPN and proxy services on the Chrome Web Store, diverting user traffic through a central SOCKS5 proxy controlled by a single entity. These extensions mimicked reputable brands such as Proton VPN and NordVPN. They collected significant user data, including destination websites and user IPs, potentially compromising privacy and security. Researchers from Socket discovered the campaign, which seemed to be pushing users towards a subscription-based VPN service in Russia. Google has removed over 200 of these extensions, but more than 500 remain.

2. **Why it matters:** This incident underscores significant vulnerabilities within browser extension ecosystems, potentially affecting millions of users unaware of the threat. By routing traffic through malicious proxies, attackers can monitor web activities, steal sensitive information, and exploit users' browsing data. The impersonation of trusted brands amplifies the risk, misleading users into downloading these extensions. This breach highlights the importance of vigilance and robust vetting processes for digital platforms like Chrome's Web Store to protect privacy and maintain trust in widely-used services.

3. **What actions should be taken:** Users should regularly audit browser extensions, especially those claiming VPN functionality, removing suspicious ones. Organizations can educate users about verifying extensions before installation and emphasize the importance of downloading them only from confirmed sources. It is advisable to regularly monitor Chrome's proxy settings to detect unauthorized changes. Furthermore, Chrome Store operators can strengthen their vetting processes by improving detection techniques for malicious extensions, ensuring more proactive identification and removal, particularly through leveraging threat intelligence and incorporating user reporting mechanisms to track similar campaigns.
