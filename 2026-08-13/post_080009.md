Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/city-forum-data-theft-attacks-target-salesforce-servicenow-portals/

**1) What happened:**
A data theft campaign, named City-Forum, is actively targeting Salesforce Experience Cloud and ServiceNow customer portals using custom tools. These attacks capitalize on overly permissive sharing rules and portal configurations, allowing attackers to access data exposed to unauthenticated guest users. The campaign has been traced to a single IP address (158.220.87.79) and a domain (city-forum.com) connected to German VPS provider Contabo. The attacks focus on both older and newer Salesforce frameworks, like Aura and Lightning Web Runtime, as well as ServiceNow portals via their native search functionality.

**2) Why it matters:**
This campaign jeopardizes the security of critical data for various sectors, including telecommunications, finance, public-sector, and enterprise software. Even though no vulnerabilities in the platforms are being exploited, misconfigured permissions pose a significant risk. Unauthorized data access and potential data breaches could lead to reputational damage, financial losses, and regulatory penalties for organizations involved. The fact that the infrastructure has been stable for more than a year indicates it may be well-coordinated and effective, heightening the urgency for targeted organizations to reassess their security practices.

**3) What actions should be taken as a result of this information:**
Organizations should audit and tighten permissions for guest-user access on their Salesforce and ServiceNow portals. Specifically, Salesforce administrators should evaluate and adjust guest-user sharing rules, object permissions, and API settings to restrict unauthorized access. For ServiceNow, evaluate search sources to ensure they require authentication for sensitive data access. Continue monitoring for unusual activity from the identified IP address and watch for signs of guest user exploitation. Encouraging greater awareness and training on proper configuration settings and access controls can also diminish exposure to such data-theft campaigns.
