**1) What happened?**

The AnonyMousKIT phishing-as-a-service (PhaaS) platform, operational since early 2024, exploits voice AI agents to phish iPhone passcodes, allowing criminals to unlock stolen Apple devices and disable Activation Lock features. It operates through a network of 506 domains and 168 storefront brands reselling stolen iPhones, gathering Apple IDs, accessing iCloud backups, and Keychain credentials. SOCRadar, a threat intelligence platform, uncovered the platform's ecosystem and recorded 200 AI-driven phishing call attempts in nine months, primarily targeting Brazil. This scheme deceives users by impersonating Apple services through emails, SMS, and calls, eliciting sensitive credentials.

**2) Why it matters?**

This event highlights the rising sophistication and automation in phishing tactics, signifying a broader ecosystem that exploits stolen devices, thus posing significant risks to personal and corporate data security. By leveraging voice AI and realistic impersonations, attackers can bypass traditional security measures, leading to unauthorized access to critical information such as iCloud backups, Keychain passwords, and sensitive corporate data. This also signals the systemic issues of inadequate consumer awareness and insufficient preventive controls, especially in geographical regions heavily targeted by such scams.

**3) What actions should be taken?**

To mitigate these risks, individuals and organizations should enhance their cybersecurity awareness, emphasizing vigilance against unsolicited contacts and skepticism of messages impersonating trusted brands. Implementing stronger endpoint security solutions and enabling additional safeguards like SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) to detect unusual patterns could help. Organizations, particularly those in targeted geographies, should conduct regular security training for employees to recognize phishing attempts. Additionally, collaborating with cybersecurity entities and law enforcement is crucial to disrupt and dismantle such phishing platforms at their operational levels.
