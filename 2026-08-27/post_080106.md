Original Article: https://www.darkreading.com/endpoint-security/novacookies-steals-microsoft-365-sessions-320-a-month

1) The article discusses a cybercrime tool called 'NovaCookies,' a hacking kit available for $320 per month, which targets Microsoft 365 sessions. It functions by stealing session cookies, allowing attackers to hijack user sessions without the need for login credentials. This method enables cybercriminals to access corporate emails and sensitive data, thus bypassing multi-factor authentication (MFA) and enforcing persistence in compromised accounts. The tool signifies a sophisticated evolution in subscription-based cybercrime services.

2) The emergence of 'NovaCookies' is significant due to its potential to greatly compromise organizational security. By exploiting session cookies, attackers effectively sidestep traditional security measures like password protection and MFA, which are core defensive strategies employed to safeguard sensitive information. As many businesses rely heavily on Microsoft 365 for daily operations, this tool poses a considerable threat to data integrity, confidentiality, and overall organizational resilience against cyber threats, highlighting a growing trend towards accessible and subscription-based cybercriminal services.

3) Organizations should consider strengthening their security posture by implementing advanced monitoring solutions capable of detecting anomalous session activities. Enhancing employee awareness and training programs to recognize phishing attempts could help prevent initial infiltrations. Using device-based access controls and more robust continuous authentication measures may reduce vulnerabilities to such attacks. Regularly reviewing and updating security policies, especially those concerning access management and session integrity, can provide additional layers of protection against evolving threats like 'NovaCookies.'
