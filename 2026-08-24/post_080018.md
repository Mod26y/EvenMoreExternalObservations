Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-infect-android-car-head-units-with-proxy-botnet-malware/

The article discusses a supply-chain attack targeting Android car head units, which uses a legitimate device-update app to spread malware. The malware enlists compromised devices into a proxy botnet or uses them for ad fraud. Kaspersky researchers attributed this operation to the MoYu group, responsible for the BadBox malware botnet. This attack marks the first documented case targeting car head units. The scheme exploited systems from DoFun, a provider of automotive software and hardware, and involved downloading malware from a legitimate system app, resulting in the head units being turned into proxy botnet nodes.

This incident emphasizes the expanding threat landscape, underscoring how even non-traditional computing devices, such as car head units, can be exploited. The attack highlights vulnerabilities in the supply chain that can have far-reaching implications, including widespread privacy breaches and misuse of resources for illicit activities. Given these units handle important functions, the attack raises concerns about the broader implications for connected vehicles and potential risks to consumer privacy and data security.

To mitigate risks from such attacks, stakeholders could enhance supply chain security by conducting regular security assessments and audits of third-party software. Developing robust incident response plans and implementing proactive threat intelligence sharing can be crucial in rapidly identifying and mitigating similar threats. Encouraging manufacturers to adopt stronger verification processes for software updates and promoting user awareness about cybersecurity best practices concerning connected cars might also reduce exposure to similar threats in the future.
