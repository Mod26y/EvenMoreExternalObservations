Original Article: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/chrome-web-store-extensions-caught-stealing-crypto-browser-data/

In this event, several Chrome and Edge browser extensions have been compromised to deploy malware, stealing cryptocurrency, sensitive browser data, and injecting malicious scripts. The campaign involves 19 malicious modules with capabilities like stealing cryptocurrency and personal data, disguising phishing pages, and recording credentials. An app security company, Socket, discovered that five extensions were bought from original creators and injected with malware during automatic updates. Despite Google's quick action to remove the extensions from the Chrome Web Store, some remained on Edge.

This situation underscores the risks associated with browser extensions, which can serve as significant attack vectors due to their access to extensive browser functionality and user data. The malicious use of trusted extensions emphasizes the need for cautious management of third-party software. It is particularly concerning for users engaged in cryptocurrency transactions and online services where sensitive information is frequently entered, as they face potential severe financial and privacy consequences.

Following this information, users should authenticate the removal of any potentially malicious extensions from their browsers and change any compromised credentials. Organizations might consider limiting the use of browser extensions through policy controls and educating users about the risks of extension permissions. It's also advisable for companies to regularly scan for compromised extensions and engage with application security firms for timely threat intelligence updates.
